# Calibration against real pole laps (24 November 2026)

The car-only lap-time model (`carsim/lapsim.py`: quasi-steady, racing line, no driver margin) against the
pole laps of 2025 and 2026 (Jolpica-F1). Before: **+21 %** on average (`2025_before.md`). After: **+1.9 %**
(2025 car vs 2025 poles) and **+1.2 %** (2026 car vs 2026 poles), spread ±2.5–2.7 %.

## What was wrong

1. **The racing line, not only the car (about half of the gap).** The line was "surveyed centreline +
   a smooth lateral offset", so it kept every few-metre wiggle of the survey (points 5 m apart), and the
   offsets were interpolated linearly between the optimised points (a kink every 4 m). The lap model takes
   the corner speed at each point, so those curvature spikes capped the corners: Monza's Roggia at
   47 km/h (real ~120), the Lesmos at ~125 km/h (real ~185). The dynamic car is not affected (it drives
   between the edges), but the reference driver follows this line.
   *Fix:* the line is now a periodic cubic spline through the optimised points themselves
   (`racing_line._line_points`). The car-only model solves it on points 8 m apart (about 1.5 car lengths)
   with the car's centre 0.5 m inside the white line (a wheel on the kerb), `lapsim.LINE_MARGIN/LINE_STEP`.
   Worth ~10 % of lap time with the calibrated car; the old 1.5 m margin alone cost ~1.5 %.
2. **Too little grip and downforce, too little drag.** Matching the poles on the old line needed >8 g at
   300 km/h (real: ~5–6 g), so the car alone could not explain it. With the line fixed:

| | before | after (2025) | after (2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ClA low / medium / high (m²) | 3.2 / 4.0 / 4.7 | 4.00 / 5.00 / 5.88 | 3.09 / 3.72 / 4.36 (corner mode) |
| CdA low / medium / high (m²) | 0.85 / 1.10 / 1.30 | 1.23 / 1.60 / 1.89 | 1.13 / 1.38 / 1.60 |
| tyre μ lateral / longitudinal (at 4 kN) | 1.70 / 1.75 | 2.38 / 2.10 | × 0.94 (narrower tyres) |
| tyre load sensitivity | −0.08 | −0.25 | −0.25 |
| downforce = weight at (medium) | 207 km/h | 185 km/h | |
| lateral g at 80 / 150 / 250 / 300 km/h (medium) | 1.7 / 2.3 / 3.5 / 4.3 | 2.7 / 3.5 / 5.2 / 5.9 | |
| Monza top speed (low wing, DRS) | 391 km/h | 357 km/h | 355 km/h (straight mode) |

Real slicks are strongly load-sensitive, which is what makes the low-speed corners need more grip while
the high-speed g stays realistic. Braking grip rises less than cornering grip (peak braking ~5–6 g
plus drag). The 2026 car ends up with ~26 % less corner-mode downforce than 2025 (the FIA aimed for ~30 %).

Applied in `params.f1_2025` / `f1_2026` only (`TRIMS`, `TYRE_2025`, `TRIMS_2026`, `TYRE_GRIP_2026`); the
generic `CarParams()` of the tests is unchanged. `PHYSICS_VERSION` 5.

Also fixed: the scripted reference driver (`drivers.LapDriver`) planned with a flat 644 kW at the wheels and
a 90 m/s (324 km/h) cap; it now uses the car's own power curve and rev-limited top speed.

## What is left

- **Outliers are track data, not the car:** Baku (−5 / −4 %) and Las Vegas (−4 %) are "geo" layouts with a
  nominal 12 m width (Baku's castle section is ~7.6 m); Melbourne, Yas Marina and Madring (+6–8 %) are
  "hybrid" or new layouts. Real widths for these would tighten the spread.
- The model is quasi-steady on a minimum-curvature (not minimum-time) line; ±2–3 % per circuit is about
  what that can resolve. Speed traces (FastF1, needs an F1 login) would let us check corner by corner.
- Medium-wing top speeds look ~10 km/h low (Bahrain 323 km/h): the medium/high drag may be a little high.
